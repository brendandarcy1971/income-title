# Slide 4 Plan: Diagnosis - What Is Actually Expensive?

## Slide Purpose
Establish that affordability failure is driven primarily by land system economics and finance-capacity pricing dynamics, not just construction cost.

## Core Claim
Housing prices are increasingly set by what households can stretch to borrow, so margins and embedded costs expand toward borrowing capacity instead of tracking fundamental delivery cost.

## Audience Outcome
After this slide, the audience should accept three points:
- Construction is only one component of final price.
- Land transformation and policy settings create large price uplifts.
- Lending capacity acts as a price ceiling signal that market participants price toward.

## 2-Minute Narrative Arc
- 0:00-0:30: Open with the misconception: build cost alone explains affordability.
- 0:30-1:15: Show the cost build-up using a waterfall from raw land to final market price.
- 1:15-1:45: Show bridge decomposition: fundamental delivery cost versus finance-capacity premium.
- 1:45-2:00: Land the policy implication: price formation is system-driven, not purely input-cost driven.

## Visual Strategy
Primary visual:
- Central waterfall chart showing step-by-step cost formation.

Secondary visual:
- Small bridge chart that splits final price into:
  - Fundamental delivery economics.
  - Finance-capacity and market-settings premium.

Why this works:
- Waterfall explains pathway and cumulative additions.
- Bridge isolates the lending-capacity mechanism in one glance.

## On-Slide Copy (Short)
Headline:
- Diagnosis: Price Formation Is Land-and-Finance Driven

Subheadline:
- Affordability pressure is not just build cost. Prices rise toward borrowing capacity under constrained supply settings.

Interpretation line under chart:
- The largest uplift occurs before and around delivery, then expands further where buyers are pushed to max deposit plus max borrow.

## Speaker Notes (2 minutes)
- We often treat construction cost as the main housing problem. The data says otherwise.
- The biggest jumps happen through land transformation, infrastructure, policy and compliance layers, then through market pricing behavior.
- Lending rules and borrowing norms push households toward maximum feasible debt. That creates a hard-to-ignore pricing anchor for sellers and developers.
- In that environment, margins and embedded costs can expand toward available finance, not just fundamental input costs.
- This is why reform must target system economics, not just build efficiency.

## Risks and Guardrails
- Risk: Overstating causality between lending rules and all price growth.
- Guardrail: Phrase as a strong contributing mechanism, not the sole cause.

- Risk: Double counting categories.

- Risk: Data challenge from audience.
- Guardrail: Label as illustrative scenario calibrated to project research ranges.
