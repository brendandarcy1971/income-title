# Slide 10 Plan: Procurement Architecture

## Slide Objective
Show the operating system of delivery: government defines product, standards, and price discipline, while multiple private contractors compete on execution.

## Time Allocation
- Target talk time: 2 minutes.
- Audience outcome: They should understand this is a rules-based procurement platform, not a single-builder program.

## Core Message
Government controls framework quality and cost settings; market competition is focused on delivery performance.

## Supporting Proof Points
- Product specification is fixed (common output standard), reducing design fragmentation.
- Open participation model allows many compliant contractors to bid.
- Price/cost disclosure creates continuing cost discipline over time.
- Multi-path construction methods can compete to one audited product outcome.

## Recommended Slide Headline
Procurement Architecture: Public Rules, Competitive Delivery

## Recommended Subheadline
Set the specification once, open the market to compliant bidders, and enforce transparent cost and quality controls.

## Visual Concept
- Primary visual: Three-lane swimlane map.
- Lane 1 (Policy/Government): product definition, standards, caps, land-release rules.
- Lane 2 (Procurement): open tender, eligibility/compliance gate, bid evaluation, contract allocation.
- Lane 3 (Construction/Delivery): multi-contractor build pathways to one common output standard.

## Layout (16:9)
- Top 18%: headline and subheadline.
- Middle 66%: horizontal swimlane system map with directional arrows.
- Bottom 16%: key takeaway strip + source note.

## On-Slide Copy (Draft)

### Swimlane 1: Government Framework
- Define fixed housing product and standards.
- Set indicative cost cap and review settings.
- Set compliance, audit, and reporting rules.
- Control serviced land release into program pipeline.

### Swimlane 2: Procurement Engine
- Open participation tender model.
- Eligibility gate: capability + compliance.
- Evaluate on price, speed, quality, scalability.
- Award across multiple contractors, not single provider.

### Swimlane 3: Delivery Pathways
- Prefab/modular delivery pathway.
- Onsite systemised pathway.
- 3D-assisted/automated pathway.
- All pathways must meet identical output standard.

### Right-Side Outcome Chips
- Open market participation
- Standardised output quality
- Cost transparency and discipline
- Scalable, repeatable delivery

### Bottom Takeaway
Government sets the rules and guardrails; private industry competes inside them to deliver the same audited housing product.

## Presenter Notes (2 minutes)
This slide is the delivery architecture.

The policy design is simple: government sets the non-negotiables, and the market competes on execution.

At the framework level, government defines the product, compliance standards, and cost-discipline settings. It also controls land release into the program, so supply is coordinated instead of speculative.

In the procurement lane, participation is open to any contractor that can meet capability and compliance gates. Contracts are awarded on price, delivery speed, quality record, and ability to scale.

In the delivery lane, different construction methods are allowed, but they must converge to the same functional output standard. So innovation is welcome, but quality and product definition are fixed.

The result is a system that uses competitive pressure to improve delivery while preserving policy control over standards and affordability outcomes.
