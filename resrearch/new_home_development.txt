# 🏡 AUSTRALIAN HOUSING ECONOMICS

## Full Cost Stack (Urban Fringe / New Housing)

***

# 1. 🎯 TYPICAL TOTAL COST (NEW HOUSE + LAND)

```
Total: ~$650k – $1.3M (major cities)
```

Breakdown:

* Land: $300k – $700k+ [\[commonslibrary.org\]](https://commonslibrary.org/resources-about-australian-housing-justice-and-unwaged-rights-campaigns/)
* Build: $300k – $700k [\[theahi.com.au\]](https://www.theahi.com.au/jun-24/moving-beyond-polite-advocacy-housing-activism-in-australia)

***

# 2. 📊 COST STACK (WHERE THE MONEY GOES)

## 🟫 Land (largest component)

```
~40–60% of total cost
```

* Driven by zoning + supply constraints [\[amnesty.org.au\]](https://www.amnesty.org.au/human-rights-act-addressing-australias-housing-crisis/)

***

## 🏗️ Construction (house itself)

```
~30–45%
```

* \~$443k average build cost [\[view.com.au\]](https://view.com.au/news/housing-affordability-crisis-deepens-as-young-australians-sound-the-alarm/)
* \~$1,500–$3,500 per m² [\[view.com.au\]](https://view.com.au/news/housing-affordability-crisis-deepens-as-young-australians-sound-the-alarm/)

***

## 🏛️ Government taxes & charges

```
~15–25%
```

Includes:

* Stamp duty
* GST
* Infrastructure fees [\[amnesty.org.au\]](https://www.amnesty.org.au/human-rights-act-addressing-australias-housing-crisis/)

***

## 🧱 Infrastructure contributions

```
$30k – $80k per lot
```

* Roads, utilities, schools [\[amnesty.org.au\]](https://www.amnesty.org.au/human-rights-act-addressing-australias-housing-crisis/)

***

## 👷 Builder margin

```
~10–20% of build cost
```

***

## 🏢 Developer margin

```
~10–25% of project
```

***

# 3. 🌱 ENGOBO → FINISHED LAND (VALUE TRANSFORMATION)

## 🔹 Raw (englobo land)

* \~$5k – $20k per future lot equivalent

***

## 🔹 After rezoning

* \~+$50k–$150k uplift

***

## 🔹 After development

* \~$170k per lot (civil works) [\[copound.com\]](https://copound.com/cost-of-living-family-australia-c13)

***

## 🔹 Final serviced lot

```
~$350k – $700k+
```

***

## 🧠 Key insight

> Most of the value comes from **rezoning + infrastructure + market pricing**, not raw land.

***

# 4. 📈 WHY PRICES RISE ABOVE COST

## ✅ 1. Market pricing (not cost-based)

```
Price = what buyers can borrow/pay
```

***

## ✅ 2. Demand > supply

* population growth
* limited land release

***

## ✅ 3. Credit expansion

* borrowing capacity drives prices

***

## ✅ 4. Risk pricing

* developers price:
  * time delays (5–15 years)
  * capital risk

***

## ✅ 5. “Finished land scarcity”

* zoned, serviced land is limited

***

# 5. 📉 WHY BACKYARDS ARE SHRINKING

## 🧠 Mechanism:

```
Land cost ↑ → lot size ↓
```

***

## Evidence:

* Block sizes shrinking over time [\[commonslibrary.org\]](https://commonslibrary.org/resources-about-australian-housing-justice-and-unwaged-rights-campaigns/)

***

## Result:

* Smaller lots
* More density
* Same total house price

***

***

# 6. 🚧 WHAT ACTUALLY DRIVES COST

## Top drivers:

### 1. Land price (BIGGEST)

* scarcity + zoning

***

### 2. Infrastructure cost

* \~$170k per lot [\[copound.com\]](https://copound.com/cost-of-living-family-australia-c13)

***

### 3. Government charges

* up to 25% of cost [\[amnesty.org.au\]](https://www.amnesty.org.au/human-rights-act-addressing-australias-housing-crisis/)

***

### 4. Construction inflation

* build costs up sharply post-COVID [\[view.com.au\]](https://view.com.au/news/housing-affordability-crisis-deepens-as-young-australians-sound-the-alarm/)

***

***

# 7. 🧠 KEY ECONOMIC INSIGHTS

## ✅ 1. Houses are NOT expensive to build — land is

* Construction ≠ main problem
* Land + policy costs dominate

***

## ✅ 2. Housing is priced by demand, not cost

Even if:

```
Cost = $300k
```

Price can be:

```
$600k+
```

***

## ✅ 3. System is supply-constrained

* zoning limits land
* infrastructure bottlenecks

***

## ✅ 4. Government is a major cost driver

Through:

* taxes
* planning
* infrastructure charging

***

***

# 8. 🔥 SIMPLE SUMMARY (BEST ONE-LINER)

> In Australia, the price of housing is primarily driven by land scarcity, planning constraints, infrastructure costs and credit-fuelled demand, with the physical construction of the house forming a minority of the total cost.
