# Slide 3 Plan: Human Consequences

## Slide Objective
Humanise the housing crisis before moving into system economics.

## Time Allocation
- Target talk time: 1.5 minutes.
- Audience outcome: They should recognize the issue as structural life-impact, not just price volatility.

## Core Message
Housing stress is reshaping family formation, security expectations, and trust that normal pathways to ownership still exist.

## Supporting Proof Points
- People describe ownership as unreachable even with work and planning.
- Interview language frames housing as a rights and fairness issue, not only a market issue.
- Cost pressure is reported across renters and owner-occupiers.

## Recommended Slide Headline
Human Consequences: What People Say the Crisis Feels Like

## Recommended Subheadline
Housing stress is changing whether households can plan stable work, family, and ownership futures.

## Visual Concept
- Primary visual: Quote wall with three short attributed quotes.
- Secondary visual: A short synthesis panel translating sentiment into policy-relevant implications.

## Layout (16:9)
- Top band: Headline and subheadline.
- Middle row: Three quote cards with attribution tags.
- Lower panel: Three synthesis bullets.
- Bottom strip: One-sentence takeaway.

## Source Inputs
Use curated, attributable quotes from [research/quotes.txt](research/quotes.txt):
- ABC News interview quote on inability to afford ownership.
- ABC News quote on housing as asset vs right.

## On-Slide Copy (Draft)
Quote cards:
- "We’ve got no hope really of being able to afford a place of our own."
- "There is a huge issue… where housing is seen as an asset, not a human right."
- "Renters and owner-occupiers are being squeezed by cost of living and the housing crisis."

Synthesis bullets:
- The problem is felt as blocked entry, not just higher prices.
- People describe housing as a social contract failure, not a normal market cycle.
- The crisis is influencing family formation, life planning, and institutional trust.

Bottom takeaway:
- If mainstream workers and renters believe normal housing milestones are gone, the issue is structural exclusion.

## Presenter Notes (1.5 minutes)
This slide is here to set the human baseline before technical diagnosis.

The quotes are short by design. They show blocked ownership expectations, a rights-versus-asset tension, and broad squeeze across tenure types.

The key point is pattern recognition: this is not one demographic complaint or a temporary market dip. It is a repeated social signal that core life milestones are breaking.

That is why the next slide shifts to mechanisms: where costs and pricing pressure are formed in the system.

## Risks and Guardrails
- Risk: Quotes read as partisan or anecdotal.
- Guardrail: Use multiple sources and keep attributions visible.

- Risk: Overweighting emotion before analysis.
- Guardrail: Keep synthesis panel concise and transition quickly to diagnostic evidence.

## Build-Ready Checklist
- Exactly three quotes, all attributable.
- Quote cards readable at room distance.
- Synthesis panel is plain-language and policy-relevant.
- End line tees up Slide 4 diagnosis cleanly.
