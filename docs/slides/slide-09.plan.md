# Slide 9 Plan: Cost Structure and Target Economics

## Slide Objective
Show that the $350k per-dwelling target is a designed, controllable outcome built from explicit cost envelopes.

## Time Allocation
- Target talk time: 2 minutes.
- Audience outcome: They should understand the target economics as a governed delivery design, not a market assumption.

## Core Message
The $350k target is produced by product standardisation and procurement discipline, with an indicative split of ~$100k serviced land and ~$250k build.

## Supporting Proof Points
- Cost target is decomposed into clear land/build envelopes.
- Assumptions are explicit and auditable (template, procurement lots, quality gates, compliance).
- Governance controls hold delivery inside envelope rather than relying on speculative market correction.

## Recommended Slide Headline
Cost Structure and Target Economics

## Recommended Subheadline
The $350k target is a designed outcome, not a hope.

## Visual Concept
- Primary visual: Stacked target bar (or donut) showing $100k land + $250k build.
- Secondary visual: Assumptions panel listing operating controls.
- Tertiary visual: Threshold check cards summarising key economics checkpoints.

## Layout (16:9)
- Top 18%: Headline + subheadline.
- Middle 66%: Left chart, right checks/assumptions panel.
- Bottom 16%: One-line takeaway and source note.

## On-Slide Copy (Draft)

### Target Stack
- Serviced land envelope: ~$100k
- Build envelope: ~$250k
- Total target delivered cost: ~$350k

### Assumptions Panel
- Standardised product spec
- Repeatable procurement lots
- Serviced land discipline
- Multi-contractor competition
- QA and compliance controls

### Bottom Takeaway
Target economics are achieved by product and procurement design, not by assuming market prices will self-correct.

## Presenter Notes (2 minutes)
This slide anchors the affordability claim in a simple designed stack.

The key point is that $350k is not presented as optimism. It is treated as a governed target with two envelopes: serviced land and construction delivery.

The assumptions panel makes clear this only works when the operating controls are explicit: a standard product, repeatable procurement lots, and enforceable quality/compliance gates.

This transitions directly to the procurement architecture slide, which explains how these controls are executed in contracts.
