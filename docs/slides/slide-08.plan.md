# Slide 8 Plan: Product Definition - What Is Being Delivered

## Slide Objective
Make the product concrete: this is not an abstract policy, it is a standard, family-suitable detached home with clear minimum specs and repeatable quality.

## Time Allocation
- Target talk time: 1.5 minutes.
- Audience outcome: They should be able to describe the product in one sentence after this slide.

## Core Message
Income Title delivers a standard, liveable detached family home product at scale, with quality and cost discipline built into specification.

NSW pattern-book learnings reinforce this approach: reusable, pre-designed templates with controlled adaptation can reduce redesign effort and speed approvals.

## Supporting Proof Points
- Product type is fixed and auditable (not open-ended bespoke builds).
- Family functionality is explicit (3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, 2 living zones).
- Form factor is practical and repeatable (single storey, approx 140-160 sqm internal area).
- Quality consistency is a procurement requirement, not a marketing claim.
- Pattern-book style reusable templates can improve speed-to-approval and design consistency.
- Controlled site adaptation is possible without losing a standardised compliance pathway.

## Recommended Slide Headline
The Product Is Defined: A Standard Family Detached Home

## Recommended Subheadline
Clear minimum specification enables quality consistency, faster delivery, and predictable economics.

## NSW Learnings To Incorporate

- Emphasise that repeatable designs can still be adapted to specific site contexts.
- Position this slide as the detached-home equivalent of a pattern-book discipline: standard core layout plus controlled variants.
- Link the product-definition logic to approval acceleration, not only construction efficiency.

### Right Panel: Minimum Product Spec

- Typical lot context: New serviced community delivery
- Internal area target: Approx 140-160 sqm
- Bedroom count: 3
- Bathroom count: 2
- Living zones: 2
- Parking baseline: At least one dedicated parking space (garage or carport) plus practical driveway access
- Outdoor baseline: Private backyard/open-space zone sized for normal family use
- Accessibility baseline: Step-free entry where feasible
- Compliance: NCC and state code compliant

### Bottom Takeaway
Defined product standards convert housing policy intent into a buildable, auditable delivery unit.

Pattern-book precedent shows this approach can also accelerate approvals when standards and adaptation rules are explicit.

## Presenter Notes (1.5 minutes)
This slide answers a simple question: what exactly are we delivering?

Income Title is not a broad subsidy for whatever the market happens to supply. It is a defined home product with fixed minimum standards.

The reason that matters is execution. A standard product improves procurement comparability, reduces design churn, and helps keep quality and cost within clear bounds.

For households, these are normal family homes: three bedrooms, two bathrooms, two living areas, single storey, and practical liveability.

For government and delivery partners, this creates a measurable unit of output. That supports faster approvals, clearer tendering, and better auditing of outcomes.

NSW's Pattern Book experience supports the design logic here: policy-backed templates can preserve quality while reducing redesign and approval friction.
