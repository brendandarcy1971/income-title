# Slide 11 Plan: 100k-Lot Operating Cycle (State-Led Acquisition)

## Slide Objective
Show a complete, delivery-realistic operating cycle for a 100,000-lot housing program where states perform compulsory acquisition of englobo land and coordinate delivery through overlapping tranches.

## Time Allocation
- Target talk time: 2 minutes.
- Audience outcome: They should understand who does what, in what order, and how throughput reaches 100k lots.

## Core Message
100,000 lots is delivered by a repeatable tranche engine: state-led land control, streamlined approvals with consent-based intergovernmental settings, staged servicing, and competitive build delivery under fixed cost/quality guardrails.

Targeted access rule: Income Title allocations are reserved for market-excluded households (for example long-term renters) under transparent eligibility and priority scoring.

## Supporting Proof Points
- 100,000 lots is broken into 10 tranches of 10,000 for manageable execution.
- New tranche launch every ~6 months creates pipeline overlap and steady output.
- State compulsory acquisition captures land transformation uplift into the public delivery system.
- Contractors compete on civil and vertical delivery inside fixed standards.
- Program access is targeted to households shut out of ownership, improving fairness and political defensibility.

## Stage Model (On-Slide)
1. Precinct declaration and corridor selection
2. Compulsory acquisition and compensation settlement
3. Program planning + code-based approvals
4. Civil servicing and utilities delivery
5. Serviced-lot release into build pipeline
6. Vertical build and QA certification
7. Occupancy, defects closeout, and tranche audit

## Responsibility Split (On-Slide)
### Commonwealth
- Funding architecture
- National standards and reporting
- Incentive settings tied to delivery performance

### State Government
- Compulsory acquisition authority
- Planning and subdivision approvals
- Utility and civil coordination
- Contracting authority for land/civil/build packages

### Contractors
- Survey, planning, and engineering
- Civil works and utility installation
- House construction under fixed specs
- Defects rectification and handover compliance

### Independent Assurance
- Cost audit
- Quality audit
- Schedule/performance verification

## Program Math (On-Slide Callout)
- Total lots: 100,000
- Tranche size: 10,000
- Number of tranches: 10
- Launch cadence: every ~6 months
- Steady state: multiple active tranches in parallel

## Targeted Eligibility Overlay (On-Slide Callout)
- Base eligibility: no current ownership, long-term rental history, income-band test.
- Priority scoring: years renting, rent stress, household need.
- Safeguards: anti-gaming restrictions and independent review.

## KPI Dashboard (On-Slide Short List)
- Cost per serviced lot
- Cost per completed dwelling
- Approval cycle time
- Serviced lots delivered per month
- On-time handover rate
- Defect rate at 90/180 days

## Presenter Notes (2 minutes)
This slide explains execution, not aspiration.

The delivery unit is the tranche: 10,000 lots per tranche, repeated 10 times. That allows disciplined sequencing while maintaining national scale.

States lead compulsory acquisition and planning control so land-value uplift is managed in the program, not left to speculative capture.

Contractors then compete on two things: civil servicing and vertical build, under fixed product standards and transparent cost controls.

Allocation is targeted, not universal: places are reserved for households demonstrably excluded from ownership, especially long-term renters under sustained rental stress.

The program reaches 100,000 not by one giant release, but by overlapping tranches with a regular launch cadence and auditable monthly KPIs.

## Risks and Guardrails
- Risk: acquisition delays from legal challenge.
- Guardrail: early corridor designation, transparent valuation protocol, and fast appeals channel.

- Risk: civil bottlenecks reduce lot-release cadence.
- Guardrail: pre-procured utility and civil panels with staged milestone controls.

- Risk: cost drift as tranches scale.
- Guardrail: monthly independent audit, open-book reporting, and reset triggers.
