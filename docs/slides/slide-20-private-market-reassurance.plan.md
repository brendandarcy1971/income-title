# Slide 20 Plan: Private Market Reassurance - Why Developers Stay In

## Slide Objective
Directly rebut the objection that large-scale public land/program coordination will force private developers to exit.

## Time Allocation
- Target talk time: 1.5 minutes.
- Audience outcome: They should see this as market reconfiguration toward delivery competition, not market elimination.

## Core Message
The model removes speculative bottlenecks, not private participation. Developers shift from windfall-dependent land arbitrage to predictable contracted delivery.

## Supporting Proof Points
- Open multi-contractor procurement remains central.
- Pipeline certainty improves workforce and capital planning.
- Payment risk reduces when approvals/land release are stabilised.
- Reasonable margins remain through competitive package design.

## Recommended Slide Headline
Will Private Developers Exit? No - They Shift to Scaled Delivery

## Recommended Subheadline
Government controls land and approvals throughput; private firms still deliver civil works and homes at scale.

## Visual Concept
- Primary visual: Before/After model.
  - Before: speculative landbank + uncertain approvals + volatile delivery.
  - After: predictable tranches + open tenders + contracted production.
- Secondary visual: 4 reassurance levers (pipeline, margins, package diversity, rollover incentives).

## Layout (16:9)
- Top 18%: headline + subheadline.
- Middle 60%: before/after comparison.
- Bottom 22%: policy guardrails and one-line rebuttal.

## On-Slide Copy (Draft)
### What Changes
- Less private capture of rezoning windfall.
- More predictable delivery revenue.
- Lower approval and holding-cost volatility.

### What Stays Private
- Survey, planning, engineering
- Civil and utility delivery
- Home construction and subcontracting
- Defects and lifecycle service work

### Policy Guardrails to Prevent Crowd-Out
- Open panel, no single-state-builder monopoly.
- Minimum private delivery share by tranche.
- Tiered packages for large, mid, and SME participants.
- Independent annual market-impact review.

### Bottom Rebuttal
This is not nationalisation of construction; it is de-risked market delivery at scale.

## Presenter Notes (1.5 minutes)
The objection assumes private activity only happens when land windfalls are available. That is too narrow.

Developers stay when margins are fair and pipeline is reliable. This model provides both through repeatable tranches, clear standards, and open competition.

The private sector still builds the homes. Government is stabilising the land-and-approval bottleneck so delivery can scale.

So the likely outcome is not exit; it is a shift from speculative gains to production-led returns.

## Data/Assumption Inputs to Verify Before Final Deck
- Confirm target private participation share by tranche.
- Confirm package sizing for Tier 1, mid-tier, and SME contractors.
- Confirm incentive settings (gainshare, performance rollover).
- Confirm independent market-impact review design.

## Risks and Guardrails
- Risk: perceived margin compression.
- Guardrail: transparent tender bands and gainshare mechanisms.

- Risk: concentration among a few large firms.
- Guardrail: package segmentation and participation quotas.

- Risk: crowd-out narrative persists politically.
- Guardrail: publish private participation KPIs and contractor diversity metrics.
