# Executive Summary: Income Title Housing Proposal

## Purpose
Income Title is a policy and delivery model designed to make detached home ownership attainable for working households by removing the two biggest barriers in the current system: deposit constraints and long-term mortgage debt burden.

## The Problem
Australia's housing market is pricing households by borrowing capacity rather than build cost. Land scarcity dynamics, planning constraints, infrastructure charging, and financing settings combine to produce high entry prices and long repayment horizons. The result is delayed household formation, reduced mobility, and growing economic insecurity.

## The Policy Response
Income Title creates a structured ownership pathway where eligible households:
- Move in immediately.
- Make income-linked payments rather than mortgage repayments.
- Convert to full freehold ownership when a defined repayment threshold is reached.

The model is paired with a procurement framework that standardises product outcomes and introduces disciplined, transparent competition on delivery cost and quality.

## Delivery Design
- Standard dwelling specification: detached 3-bedroom homes, family-suitable layout, consistent quality standard.
- Target cost structure: approximately $350,000 all-in per dwelling (indicative: serviced land plus build).
- Procurement model: government defines product and standards; private sector competes through open participation under cost and compliance rules.
- Construction flexibility: prefab, onsite systemised, and emerging automated methods can all participate if they meet the same output standard.

## Household Economics
Repayments are linked to household income (indicatively 10-12%) with clear conversion rules to full title. This avoids compounding interest dynamics and aligns repayment burden with earning capacity over time. Under reasonable wage growth assumptions, typical pathways can converge to ownership in approximately 10-15 years for many cohorts, with longer pathways still materially better than conventional mortgage stress settings.

## Government Economics
Income Title should be treated as a targeted policy investment rather than a pure subsidy transfer. A significant share of delivery cost is recovered through structured repayments, supporting a revolving funding profile over time. Fiscal settings can be tuned through pilot evidence on recovery rates, delivery costs, and household outcomes.

## Why This Is Credible
The policy logic adapts proven Australian mechanisms:
- Income-linked repayment principles (HECS-style).
- Structured purchaser discipline and disclosure logic (PBS-style procurement principles).

## Risks and Controls
Key risks include delivery quality, legal complexity, market distortion concerns, and implementation coordination across jurisdictions. These can be mitigated through:
- Clear title architecture and transfer rules.
- Robust contractor accreditation and quality assurance.
- Transparent cost disclosure and benchmarking.
- Phased pilot-to-scale rollout with explicit decision gates.

## Recommended Next Step
Commission a cross-jurisdiction design and pilot package immediately:
- 100-day design phase (legal, financial, procurement, and governance settings).
- 12-month pilot delivery in selected growth corridors.
- Independent evaluation framework tied to affordability, repayment performance, and scalability.

Income Title is a practical, testable path to expand ownership access while retaining fiscal discipline and delivery accountability.
