## Brief: Income Title Proposal Storyline

## References

Use research from `research/`.

## Core Narrative

Develop a proposal for an "income title" model as a practical response to Australia's housing affordability and supply crisis.

The storyline should cover:

- Background: the severity and urgency of the housing crisis, supported by strong data points and quotes.
- Precedents: where similar public-policy mechanics have worked (for example, HECS income-linked repayment, PBS single-buyer procurement logic).
- Root problem: land and building economics, including why current settings push costs beyond reach.
- Financial model: funding structure, target cost per home, repayment mechanisms, and fiscal implications.
- Mobility and lifecycle: transferability, disposal, and how people move between homes over time.
- Title architecture: how the income title works in legal and practical terms.
- Procurement and approvals: what governments should set, what is procured, by whom, and through which approval pathway.

## Audience

- Major Australian think tanks (for example Chifley and Menzies).
- Politicians and senior policy advisers.

## Presentation Format

- A sharp, pithy 45-minute presentation.
- Slides should include suggested visuals and imagery.
- Language should be clear and direct.
- Use charts where useful (for example waterfall charts, comparison bridges, and simple system diagrams).
- Minimise text-heavy slides except where precision is required.
- Include a slide-by-slide outline covering message, evidence, imagery, and suggested layout.

## Suggested Approach to Generate the Plan

1. Define the single headline promise.
	- One sentence: what income title changes, for whom, and why now.

2. Build a 3-part narrative arc.
	- Problem now.
	- Why current mechanisms fail.
	- How income title solves the failure with manageable risk.

3. Translate the narrative into a slide map.
	- Assign each section in "Core Narrative" to 2-4 slides.
	- Target 18-24 slides total for 45 minutes, including Q&A buffer.

4. Gather evidence by section.
	- For each section, collect: 3 key facts, 1 quote, 1 visual.
	- Pull from `research/` and tag each item by source and confidence.

5. Define the policy mechanics clearly.
	- Specify eligibility, repayment trigger, governance, transfer/disposal rules, and default safeguards.
	- Make each rule testable with a short example household scenario.

6. Build the financial logic as a simple model.
	- Show target build cost, subsidy/finance split, repayment profile, and budget impact ranges.
	- Use base, conservative, and stretch scenarios.

7. Design the government implementation path.
	- What Commonwealth controls.
	- What states/local government control.
	- Procurement model, approvals reform, and sequencing.

8. Stress-test likely objections.
	- Market distortion, fairness, fiscal risk, legal complexity, and political feasibility.
	- Prepare one rebuttal slide per major objection.

9. Finish with an action plan.
	- 100-day actions, 12-month pilot, and 3-year scale pathway.
	- Include decision points and required stakeholders.

10. Final quality pass.
	- Remove jargon.
	- Ensure every slide answers: "So what?"
	- Keep a consistent visual language and pace.
