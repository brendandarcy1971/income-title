# Income Title Proposal: 45-Minute Speaking Script Outline

## Delivery Notes
- Tone: direct, policy-literate, solutions-focused.
- Style: short sentences, low jargon, high clarity.
- Pace: approximately 130-145 spoken words per minute.
- Target script length for full delivery: approximately 5,200-5,800 words.

## 1) Opening and Framing (Minutes 0:00-3:00)
### Objective
- Establish urgency and define the promise.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "Australia has a housing access problem, not just a construction problem."
- "Today I will show a model that removes deposit barriers, reduces long debt exposure, and keeps fiscal discipline."
- "The proposal is called Income Title: occupy now, repay by income, own fully on completion."

### Transition
- "First, we need to be precise about what is broken in the current system."

## 2) Problem Definition (Minutes 3:00-9:00)
### Objective
- Show why current economics lock out working households.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "Housing prices are being set by financing capacity and constrained land systems, not by efficient delivery cost alone."
- "When land, infrastructure and charges compound, households face a deposit-plus-mortgage cliff."
- "This is changing family formation, job mobility, and long-term economic confidence."

### Evidence Cues
- Use one chart on housing costs vs incomes.
- Use 1-2 quotes to humanise impacts.

### Transition
- "If we diagnose the system correctly, the policy response becomes clearer."

## 3) Policy Logic and Precedents (Minutes 9:00-14:00)
### Objective
- Build confidence through familiar Australian policy mechanics.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "We already use income-linked repayment in HECS."
- "We already use disciplined purchasing frameworks in PBS-type settings."
- "Income Title combines those logics for housing delivery and ownership progression."

### Transition
- "Now I will walk through exactly how the model works."

## 4) Model Mechanics (Minutes 14:00-22:00)
### Objective
- Explain occupancy, repayment, title conversion, and mobility rules.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "Entry is immediate occupancy under defined title conditions."
- "Repayment is a set proportion of income, not an interest-compounding mortgage."
- "When the repayment threshold is met, title converts automatically to freehold."
- "If a household moves earlier, transfer and disposal rules settle remaining balance transparently."

### Clarity Checks to Say Out Loud
- "This is not rent."
- "This is not a grant."
- "This is a structured ownership pathway with clear legal conversion."

### Transition
- "Mechanics matter, but delivery credibility matters just as much."

## 5) Delivery and Procurement (Minutes 22:00-29:00)
### Objective
- Show how government and market roles are separated cleanly.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "Government sets the product, standards and cost discipline."
- "The private sector competes on execution speed, quality and cost."
- "Multiple build pathways are allowed, but output standards are fixed."

### Key Points to Land
- Standard housing spec keeps quality consistent.
- Open participation and benchmarking reduce cost drift.
- Cost disclosure supports evidence-based cap management.

### Transition
- "With delivery established, let's look at household and fiscal outcomes."

## 6) Household and Fiscal Outcomes (Minutes 29:00-35:00)
### Objective
- Demonstrate practical affordability and policy sustainability.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "Income-linked repayments align burden with earning capacity over time."
- "Wage growth can accelerate completion for many cohorts."
- "Government outlays are partially recovered through structured payments, creating recycling potential."

### Evidence Cues
- Walk one moderate-income and one middle-income trajectory.
- Use a simple cashflow bridge for public finance.

### Transition
- "No policy is complete without confronting risks directly."

## 7) Risks, Objections, and Mitigations (Minutes 35:00-40:00)
### Objective
- Pre-empt pushback and show design maturity.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "Concern one: distortion. Response: bounded eligibility, transparent rules, pilot-based scaling."
- "Concern two: legal complexity. Response: clear title stages and conversion triggers codified up front."
- "Concern three: quality. Response: accredited delivery panel and independent quality assurance."
- "Concern four: fiscal risk. Response: scenario modelling, cap discipline, and staged expansion."

### Transition
- "The final question is not whether this is perfect. It is whether it is testable, governable, and better than status quo drift."

## 8) Decision Ask and Close (Minutes 40:00-45:00)
### Objective
- End with concrete decisions and timing.

### Talk Track Prompts
- "Approve a 100-day design package across legal, financial, procurement and governance streams."
- "Launch a 12-month pilot in selected growth corridors with independent evaluation."
- "Use pilot data to decide 3-year scale-up settings."
- "If we want more ownership, more stability, and less structural stress, we need to test this now."

## Q&A Anchor Responses (Use as needed)
- "How is this different from social housing?" -> "It is an ownership pathway with conversion to freehold, not a permanent rental tenure model."
- "How do you prevent misuse?" -> "Eligibility controls, transfer rules, and audited payment administration."
- "What if incomes fall?" -> "Income-linked structure absorbs volatility better than fixed mortgage servicing."
- "Why should government intervene this deeply?" -> "Because current settings produce persistent market failure on ownership access and long-run social cost."

## Speaker Preparation Checklist
- Rehearse to 40 minutes plus 5 minutes buffer.
- Pre-mark optional cuts (Slides 11, 14, 20) if discussion expands.
- Keep one backup slide each for legal structure and financial assumptions.
- Prepare a one-sentence answer to: "What is the single biggest reason this will work?"
